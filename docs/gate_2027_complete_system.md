### Mock Analysis Protocol (2-Hour Session)

Follow this exact process after every mock:

```
STEP 1 (15 min): Score breakdown by subject.
  → Fill in the table: Subject | Attempted | Correct | Wrong | Marks | Accuracy%

STEP 2 (30 min): Classify every wrong answer:
  → Category A: Concept gap (didn't know the method)
  → Category B: Silly mistake (knew the concept, messed up execution)
  → Category C: Time pressure (knew how to solve, ran out of time)
  → Category D: Misread question (reading comprehension failure)

STEP 3 (15 min): Log all Category A and B errors into Error Log with full solutions.

STEP 4 (45 min): Rework every wrong question from scratch (closed book).

STEP 5 (15 min): Update your "Weak Topics" list. Adjust next 3 days' revision accordingly.
```

### Most-Tested PYQ Topics

### Common Traps

- **Fourier vs. Laplace**: Forgetting that FT exists only when ROC includes jω-axis
- **DT vs. CT properties**: Some Fourier properties differ between continuous and discrete — don't interchange blindly

## 1. Handling Motivation Dips & Burnout

### The "3-Day Rule"
Everyone has bad days. If you have a bad study day, that's normal. If you have **3 consecutive bad days**, it's a signal — not of failure, but of your brain demanding a strategic reset.

**Protocol:**
```
Day 1 bad → Normal. Push through. Do at least 50% of your planned work.
Day 2 bad → Reduce load. Do only PYQs and revision (no new topics).
Day 3 bad → STOP. Take a full rest day. No guilt. Sleep, eat well, go outside.
Day 4     → Start with the EASIEST subject you enjoy most. Rebuild momentum.
```

### Burnout Prevention Checklist (Weekly)
- [ ] Sleeping 7+ hours per night (non-negotiable — sleep is when memory consolidates)
- [ ] Exercising 30+ min at least 4 days/week (even walking counts)
- [ ] Eating 3 proper meals (no skipping meals to "save time")
- [ ] Taking Sunday afternoon fully off (no study guilt)
- [ ] Talking to at least one friend/family member about non-GATE topics
- [ ] Not comparing your progress with anyone else's claimed progress

### The "Progress Anchor" Technique
Every Sunday, write down **3 specific things you learned this week** that you didn't know 7 days ago. Keep a running list. On bad days, read this list. It is concrete proof that you are moving forward, even when it doesn't feel like it.

### The "Future Self" Letter
Write a letter to yourself from the perspective of March 2027 — the version of you who has checked the GATE results and sees a score of 670. Describe how it felt, what IIITB campus looks like, what your first day of class was like. Read this letter whenever motivation drops. This is not wishful thinking — it's neuroscience. Visualization of success activates the same neural pathways as actual achievement, strengthening the behavioral patterns that lead there.

## 4. Mid-Exam Panic Recovery (The "Reset" Technique)

This is for the moment — and it WILL happen — when you hit 3–4 hard questions in a row and feel the panic rising. Your heart rate spikes. Your mind blanks. You think "I'm going to fail."

### The 60-Second Reset Protocol

```
STEP 1 (10 sec): PUT DOWN THE PEN. Close your eyes.

STEP 2 (20 sec): Breathing — 3 deep breaths.
                  In through nose (4 sec) → Out through mouth (6 sec).
                  This is not optional. This is neuroscience.
                  Slow exhales activate the vagus nerve and reduce
                  fight-or-flight response within 20 seconds.

STEP 3 (10 sec): Self-talk. Say internally:
                  "I have already solved 30+ questions correctly.
                   These hard questions are designed to rattle me.
                   I choose not to let them. I will move on."

STEP 4 (10 sec): PHYSICALLY SKIP to the next question.
                  Flag the hard ones. Come back later with fresh eyes.

STEP 5 (10 sec): Find an EASY question — any question you can solve quickly.
                  Solve it. Get a small win. Momentum is restored.
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is not weakness. This is strategy.** Every GATE topper has experienced mid-exam panic. The difference is that they have a protocol for it, and you now do too. Panic is a physiological response — it is defeated by physiological techniques (breathing), not by willpower alone.

### The "Sunk Cost" Rule
If you've spent 4 minutes on a question and you're not close to the answer: **FLAG AND MOVE ON.** The marks you "might" earn on that hard question are far less valuable than the marks you WILL earn on the 2–3 easier questions you can solve in the same time. This is mathematically proven — your expected value is higher by moving on.
